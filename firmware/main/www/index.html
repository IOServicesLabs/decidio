What moves

Close-up: nothing moves

The eye looks at the whole camera frame as 40 x 30 points and finds what moves against the background. A box is a figure it follows from frame to frame; click a box to keep following that one. The small close-up shows the largest figure, or the one you follow, cut from each frame. With an OV3660 camera the board also zooms the sensor itself onto that figure every 4 seconds, for one frame: the sharp close-up shows detail that the normal picture does not have. The model and the eye get no picture for approximately 0.6 seconds each time. It sees movement only: a person who stands still fades out. Rules use it as eye_moving, eye_figures, eye_approach, eye_new, eye_changed and eye_self_motion.

Ideas

Each idea is a set of rules for this model. Adding one saves it straight away; several can run together (up to 16 rules).

Devices this board switches

Smart Life (Tuya) devices on the same Wi-Fi. The board talks to the device itself; nothing goes through the internet. A rule switches a device when you pick it under "Switch a device" on the Ideas tab, or with the buttons here.

A camera sees nothing in a dark room, so "on while someone is in the room" cannot switch a light on at night by itself. "Also on movement" adds the radio movement score, which works in the dark but also sees pets, fans and movement behind walls.

Devices heard on the network

The name comes from your router or from the device. Many devices give a general name, for example lwip0 or ESP_353DA3 (the last 6 characters of its MAC address). If the name does not tell you which device it is, use Blink. Also, you can compare the id with the Virtual ID in the Smart Life app: open the device, click the pencil, then Device Information.

To find which device an id is, click Try, type its local key and click Blink. The device goes to the other state for 2 seconds, then back. Nothing is saved until you click Use and Save device.

CAUTION: Do not try a device that must stay on, for example a refrigerator, a pump or a medical device. Try switches it.

Add a device

The on/off data point is 1 for most plugs and switches and 20 for most newer bulbs. After saving, press Read: the device lists its data points, and the one that is true or false is the switch.

Where the local key comes from

Each device has a secret local key that only your Smart Life account can show. Make a free account at iot.tuya.com, create a cloud project, and link your Smart Life app to it (Devices, Link App Account, scan the code with the app). Then on a computer: pip install tinytuya and python -m tinytuya wizard. It prints every device with its id and key. A key changes when the device is removed from the app and paired again.

The key is stored on this board, unencrypted, and is never shown again.

This board

Group

To make a group, press Add to the group on a board in the list below. A password is not necessary. The boards of a group tell each other which of their rules are on, so a rule here can depend on a rule in another room, and this page can show and set up every board (the Group tab, and the Board list at the top). Boards that are near each other also talk straight from radio to radio (ESP-NOW), which is faster than through the router. It all stays on your own network.

If you save a group password, all the boards of the group get it, and the page of each board then asks for it (user name decidio). Without a password, each person on your network can open the pages, unless a board has a page password of its own. The password is stored unencrypted and is sent in clear when you add a board from here and when the browser logs in.

Low power (battery)

Low power is for a board on a battery in a place without a power socket. The board sleeps, and wakes some times a day to look for a few seconds. It is off until you switch it on. Read What changes below first.

Currents (estimates, not measured)

These numbers are estimates for an ESP32-S3 camera board. They were not measured on your board. A USB power meter or an INA219 sensor gives the real values. Type them here for a better estimate.

What changes when low power is on

  1. The board looks only at points in time. It sees nothing between two wakes. If something happens and stops between two wakes, the board does not see it.
  2. Each wake starts the board again. The camera adjusts its light for 1 second. Then the board looks for the look time and does what its rules say.
  3. This page works only while the board is awake: for 5 minutes after you connect the power or push the reset button, for 30 seconds at each check-in, and while a computer is connected to its USB socket. Changes to the settings also need the board awake.
  4. Alerts come some seconds late, because Wi-Fi must connect first. The board does not keep alerts to send them together. The cooldown of each rule still works.
  5. These are off: the fly eye, the Wi-Fi movement score and continuous sound. Sound questions hear only the look time.
  6. Conditions on rules of other boards are always off. The other boards see the rules of this board as not known while it sleeps.
  7. This board does not send the alerts of other boards in the group. It gives its alerts to the board that sends them, or sends them itself. It sends alerts with a photo itself.
  8. Pins that rules set go back to off while the board sleeps.
  9. Smart Life devices switch slowly, because Wi-Fi and the device connection start at each wake.
  10. for and hold of a rule count the time between wakes: when two wakes see a condition, the board counts it as true for all the time between them.
  11. The clock is set at check-ins. The wake hours and the daily summary use it. The daily summary goes at the first wake after its time.
  12. The battery estimate is an estimate. The camera of these boards stays on in standby while the board sleeps, so the real current can be higher.
  13. The board loads new firmware or a new model only while it is awake.

Battery voltage (optional)

Connect two equal resistors (for example 2 x 220 kOhm) in series from the battery + to GND. Connect their middle to a free pin from 1 to 10. The board then shows battery_v and battery_pct as inputs.

Radio movement

-

How much the Wi-Fi signal from your router changed over the last 2 seconds (0 = perfectly still), with the last 2 minutes drawn below it. A body moving between the router and the board raises it. It sees movement; it cannot tell someone sitting still from an empty room.

To set a rule's threshold: note the score with the room empty, then with someone walking, and pick a value between. Rules use it as radio_motion.
